A big model behind an API, measured

A 27B on the user's own network, answering our 14 game cases and 400 held-out pairs at three levels of access. The question is not whether it scores higher. It is whether it answers what our tables miss.

TL;DR

  • It rescues about a quarter of what we miss. English next-word: the engine misses 109 of 200 rows, the beam answers 26 of them. Coverage at a strip of six goes from 45.5% to 58.5%.
  • It beats our tables nowhere. Every hit@1 and hit@3 column goes to the engine, both languages, all three access levels.
  • The rescues are semantic. "it's no place for a" wants woman; we offer while, long, minute, because we know for a while.
  • A merge must add, never replace. The beam also loses 31 rows the engine had. The union is worth 13 points; a swap is worth less than nothing.
  • The re-ranker cannot rescue anything. Its candidates are our own six chips, so its rescue rate is zero by construction, and its order is worse than ours at every blend weight.
  • Czech rescues half as often as English. 10.3% against 23.9%, the same split this project keeps meeting.
The verdict in one line. Worth having as a second opinion that adds candidates. Not worth having anywhere near the ranking.
Run-to-run spread. This server does not repeat itself exactly: six runs of the same 200 English rows gave 17.5 to 20.0 hit@1. Read every number as plus or minus a point. One early run showed 37.0 hit@3, beating the engine's 35.0; no repeat came near it, including repeats of that exact code. That "win" is withdrawn.

Next-word is where the rescue lives, and it is the task this keyboard values most: a gesture letter costs more than a tap letter, so the chip after a space saves more here than on a normal keyboard. The beam rescues more than chat, 26 against 16, and that gap is the interface. Prefix-2 barely moves, because the engine already covers 84% of those rows.

Every one is a sentence where the last two words decide nothing and the whole clause decides everything. for a really is followed by while most of the time. Only the sentence says otherwise. This is the ceiling the roadmap calls the honest one, and a big model is over it.

Two limits, both pushing the same way. The pair dump keeps only targets inside our vocabulary, so this cannot see the out-of-vocabulary tail, which is where the game says the model is strongest. And each figure is one run. The true rescue rate is not lower than this; it is probably higher.

The corrupted prefix is a collapse in every mode: 3.1 against 35.4. Two letters with one of them wrong is a job for edit distance over a known lexicon, not for meaning.

A correction about the beam. The first version put the typed letters into the prompt, so the beam only ever saw the distribution after "you are am" and never the one after "you are". That cost 35 points of English prefix hit@1, and this note first blamed the server's 20-logprob cap. Measured properly the cap is mostly not the problem: the top 20 after the context holds a prefix-compatible token in 84 of 100 rows, and the exact target word in 51 of 100 English rows.

The re-ranker, blended

Czech behaves the same way. The result is structural rather than about this model: a re-ranker sees only what the generator already found, so it cannot rescue a single row, and on the rows the generator got right, three tables of counts already order them better than a 27B does. The value of a big model here is entirely in the candidates it adds.

What this changes for direction 8

  • The direction is confirmed and the price is known. A second opinion answering 24% of our English next-word misses is worth having. One that ranks the strip is not.
  • The merge must be additive and selective. The beam loses 31 rows for every 26 it rescues. Add its candidates to ours, never replace ours, then decide which of the twelve to show. That decision rule is the open work.
  • English and Czech are not the same problem. 23.9% against 10.3%, which supports the two-specialist plan again.
  • Direction 5 loses its most likely candidate. The biggest model this project can reach fails to reorder six chips better than the tables that produced them.
  • Reasoning models are the wrong tool. Use the non-thinking path or a base model.

Still open on this hardware

Measure the rescue rate on the tail. The pair dump excludes out-of-vocabulary targets by construction, cutting out exactly the case the game says this model wins. A pair set without that filter decides how much of direction 7 a served model could replace.

Raise the logprob cap and re-measure. vLLM's --max-logprobs defaults to 20. The 84% figure says it is not the main limit, but the remaining 16% of rows are invisible to the beam today, and the rescue rate is what would grow. A server restart, no new code.

Find the decision rule. Engine plus beam is 58.5% where the engine alone is 45.5%. Everything between those two numbers depends on knowing when to trust the model, and that is unmeasured.
