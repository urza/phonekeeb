How deep the word list stays a word list

1350 words sampled at 54 depth bands, then labelled one by one, to find where each language stops being language.

  • Cut English at 50000 and Czech at 150000. One size for both languages is wrong in either direction.
  • The number moves three times. wordfreq's order says English 30000. The blend with subtitles says 50000. The words that survive the aspell gate confirm 50000: the last 10000 of them are 56% good and 4% bad.
  • English breaks early. In wordfreq's own order it falls from 68% to 40% good in one step at 20000. In the blended order that ships, the same cliff waits until about 50000.
  • Czech does not break until 200000. It is still 84% good at 150000, because the words down there are ordinary words in a case the shallow ranks never reached.
  • Czech tolerates three to five times the depth of English. One Czech verb spreads over 18000 ranks. The English equivalent finishes inside 300.
  • aspell cannot answer where to cut. It calls waukesha a word and železnička junk, so it reads as loose in English and tight in Czech. It is still a good junk filter at depth, which is a different job.

What was measured

tools/vocab-depth.py takes wordfreq's large list per language and applies the build script's structural filters: same alphabet regex, same one-letter whitelist, same DROP set. That leaves 307604 English and 596230 Czech candidates in frequency order.

The script samples 25 random words per band and writes them with no verdict attached. Claude labelled all 1350 by hand: 900 in wordfreq's order, 350 in the blended order the build script ranks by, and 100 in the finished lists:

  • Good: the strip should offer this word.
  • Marginal: real, but an obscure name, a technical term, or an established foreign word. It costs a prefix slot and rarely helps.
  • Bad: a typo, a form with the diacritics stripped, a fragment, an abbreviation, or a word from the other language.

cum is the share of running text the list covers down to that band, from wordfreq's own probabilities. It is the value side of the trade: a band full of junk costs nothing if it carries no text either.

Czech carries a different junk class instead of English's names: words from the other language. you sits at rank 1400, then high, day, from, young, friendly, paris, italy. English words inside a Czech list are the worst entry this keyboard can hold, because both languages share one strip.

Percentages are good words. The blend moves the English cliff from 20000 out to about 50000, and leaves Czech where it was.

That asymmetry is the same finding again. The junk the blend removes is junk subtitles never contain: web names, brands, handles, transliterations. English carries a mountain of those, so demanding subtitle support repairs the English tail. Czech never had that problem, so nothing changes. What English gains at 15000 to 30000 is ordinary speech that wordfreq under-ranked: gator, autism, beetles, nemesis, forgave, socialize, dugout, rappers.

The blended English tail past 50000 still fails the same way: tanguy, rossendale, kleist, bandra, mcelwaine, hollyweird, amzn, ilb. The blended Czech tail at 150000 to 200000 is still ordinary Czech: prodání, nevycházej, rozzářený, uhodíte, blankytná, meditačních, nastupovaly.

The gate moves the number

The build script drops each candidate that fails its aspell gate, its cross-language guard, or needs_apostrophe(). CORRECTION

The first version of this note recommended English 30000. That read the candidate stream, and the keyboard ships the filtered list. English 50000 is the right cut, and the gate is what makes it safe. The last 10000 entries cost 40% marginal and one bad word in 25, not the 24% bad the raw curve predicts at that depth.

A position in this section is a position in the cut order, the blended ranking that decides which words are in the list. It is not a position in the shipped file. The file carries a different order on purpose: the count it stores is the OpenSubtitles probability alone, so the strip ranks by spoken frequency (you first, hello at 210, playlist at 48906). Ranking the strip by the blend was tried and reverted, because it pushed you off the neutral strip and hello out of reach of the typo helo. This section is a membership measurement, which is what a cut decision needs. Its four bands name sets of words, not neighbourhoods in the file: in the count order the English sample above is spread from position 14126 to 49777.

What the cuts should be

English: 50000. Measured on the words that survive the gate, cut-order 30000–40000 is 80% good and 40000–50000 is 56% good with one bad word in 25. Position 50000 reaches stream rank 63793, where the gate is doing most of the work. The concrete win at that depth is the word that started this: playlists, prediction game case 16. Marginal entries are cheaper than they look, because a rank-45000 word is too rare to outrank a good word on a short prefix. It surfaces only on a long prefix where nothing better matches. Do not go past 50000: the stream at 75000 is 8% good, and no gate rescues mcelwaine, hollyweird and amzn.

Czech: 150000. The surviving Czech words are 88% good at cut-order 100000–125000 and 100% good at 125000–150000. Ranks 40000 to 150000 carry 5.8% of Czech running text, more than the whole English 20000-to-300000 tail carries. Czech at 40000 was too shallow by a wide margin. 150000 covers 95.7% of Czech tokens and still has headroom by this measurement, so going deeper later is defensible. There is no reason to do it yet.

Two junk classes no depth cut removes

English contractions without the apostrophe. havent sits near rank 14000, inside any English cut. The build script already rejects these with needs_apostrophe(), and the sample confirms the rule earns its place.

Czech words with the diacritics stripped. nove, svuj, ted, neni, nevim, myslim, muj, kdyz. People type without diacritics and wordfreq counts the result as a word. The class grows with depth: 0.1% of the top 5000, 0.7% at 100000–150000, 1.5% at the bottom. About 796 of them sit in the top 150000.

DO NOT SHIP THE OBVIOUS FILTER

Rejecting a plain form whose accented twin is 20 times more frequent also kills rad (genitive plural of rada), cech (a guild), dum, site and par. aspell cannot rescue them either: aspell cs itself carries mam, novy, proc and dobry. The class is worth about 0.5% of a 150000-word Czech list, and removing it needs a real Czech lexicon, not a strip rule.

What depth cannot buy

železnička sits at rank 366885, below the point where the Czech list is more junk than language. No cut reaches it without taking the junk too. The same holds for any word that is rare in the corpus but common for one person. That is the personal model's job, not the vocabulary size's. Depth fixes words that are common everywhere and merely late in the ranking. It does not fix words that are common only for you.

Caveats

  • 25 words per band is a small sample. Each rate carries about 10 percentage points of error. The headline gap is far larger than that (English 40% good against Czech 92% in the same 20000–30000 band), but no single band should be read to the percent.
  • The labels are one judgement, made by Claude, not a panel. Good and bad are firm. Marginal is the fuzzy class, and it is where another reader would disagree most.
  • wordfreq's data stops at about 2021 and will not be updated, so the tail carries the internet of that time.
